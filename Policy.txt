Absolutely. This architecture represents an automated Insurance Policy Document Intake & Policy Intelligence system. The main idea is:

A user uploads an insurance policy document → Flask receives it → AWS stores and processes it → extracted information is structured → Snowflake stores and analyzes it → users/business teams can search, track, and analyze policies.

Let me break the entire use case down from business perspective → technical architecture → end-to-end flow.

The Flask application doesn't need to perform every heavy processing operation itself.

Flask handles requests and business logic; AWS handles storage, asynchronous processing, notifications and infrastructure services.

    ↓

50. How to explain this in an interview

If someone asks:

"Explain your project architecture."

You can give this answer:

"This project is an automated insurance policy document intake and intelligence platform. Users or insurance agents upload policy documents through a Flask REST API. Flask handles authentication, validation, metadata and business logic. The documents are stored in Amazon S3, and an event-driven workflow using EventBridge triggers AWS Lambda for document processing. Lambda extracts and validates important policy information and stores operational metadata and processing status in DynamoDB. Notifications are handled through SNS and the entire workflow is monitored using CloudWatch. Snowflake provides search-ready views and analytical capabilities for dashboards, reporting and business intelligence. This ultimately enables faster policy lookup, automated document processing, better tracking, auditability and data-driven insurance operations."
